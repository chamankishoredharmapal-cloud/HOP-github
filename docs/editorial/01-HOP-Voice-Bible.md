# House of Padmavati: Voice Bible

This document is the foundational text defining how House of Padmavati (HOP) speaks. It governs every written interaction, from a single button label to a thousand-word editorial. 

Before consulting specific usage rules or grammar guidelines, all writers must understand the philosophy and rhythm defined here.

## 1. Brand Personality

House of Padmavati speaks like a specific person: a woman named Padmavati. She is not a brand invention; she is real. Her voice is the House's voice. 

The House is defined by five core personality traits:

1. **Reserved**
   - *Description:* We do not shout to be heard. We speak quietly, requiring the listener to lean in. We are comfortable with silence and white space.
   - *In Practice:* Short sentences. No exclamation marks. We never demand attention or action.
2. **Warm**
   - *Description:* Reserved does not mean cold. We are deeply hospitable. We welcome the reader into our home and treat her with profound respect.
   - *In Practice:* We use "You" directly. We express gratitude gracefully. We are never aloof or corporate.
3. **Knowledgeable**
   - *Description:* We are custodians of an ancient craft. We speak with quiet authority born of generational experience, but we never lecture.
   - *In Practice:* We explain specific techniques clearly. We name the weavers. We are precise in our terminology. 
4. **Discerning**
   - *Description:* We edit ruthlessly. Every word, like every thread, must have a purpose. We reject the superfluous and the generic.
   - *In Practice:* We avoid marketing superlatives (e.g., "stunning", "exquisite"). We let the craft stand on its own merit.
5. **Gentle**
   - *Description:* We respect the reader's time and agency. We invite; we never command. 
   - *In Practice:* We never use urgency tactics ("Hurry", "Shop Now"). We use soft imperatives ("Explore", "Discover").

## 2. Tone of Voice Dimensions

Our voice remains consistent, but our tone subtly adjusts across these six dimensions:

- **Formality (3/5):** We are respectful and structured, yet deeply personal. We avoid slang and contractions like "gonna", but we reject sterile corporate speak. We strike a balance akin to a handwritten letter from a respected mentor.
- **Warmth (4/5):** We are profoundly human. We care about the woman wearing the cloth and the hands that wove it.
- **Sensory Density (4-5/5):** This is our defining characteristic. We do not write about "luxury"; we write about the weight of the silk, the scent of jasmine, the friction of the zari.
- **Technical Depth (2/5):** We know the intricate mechanics of the loom, but we share only what enhances the reader's appreciation. We do not overwhelm with jargon. 
- **Urgency (0/5):** We operate on generational time. We never rush. We never create artificial scarcity or countdowns.
- **Imperative Force (1/5):** We do not tell the user what to do. We offer suggestions, pathways, and invitations.

## 3. Emotional Positioning

Most digital fashion brands position themselves around status, trends, or conspicuous consumption. 

**House of Padmavati positions itself around quietness, memory, and continuance.**

We do not sell a garment for an event; we offer a living artifact for a lifetime. When a woman interacts with HOP, she should feel an emotional arc moving from *Arrival* to *Curiosity*, through *Wonder* and *Trust*, settling ultimately into *Confidence*, *Ownership*, *Belonging*, and *Legacy*. 

We do not make her feel she is missing out. We make her feel she has arrived exactly where she belongs.

---

## 4. Communication Philosophy

**"No noise. Only the cloth."**

Our communication exists entirely to serve the cloth and the woman who wears it. Anything that distracts from the handwoven reality—marketing fluff, aggressive sales pitches, loud graphics, generic platitudes—is noise. 

We strip away the noise. We trust that if we describe the work truthfully, specifically, and beautifully, the work will resonate with the right woman.

---

## 5. Voice Spectrum

While the core personality never changes, our voice modulates slightly depending on the context:

- **Product Pages:** The focus is extreme sensory density and specific craft details. The tone is reverent, factual, and poetic.
- **Journal/Editorial:** The focus expands to culture, weaver stories, and history. The tone is narrative, expansive, and deeply knowledgeable.
- **Utility (Cart, Checkout):** The focus is clarity and respect. We maintain warmth but increase precision. We do not try to be overly clever or poetic when the user needs to complete a task. 
- **Customer Care (Email/Support):** The focus is pure warmth, resolution, and gentleness. We are accommodating, apologetic if necessary, and entirely human.

## 6. Sentence Rhythm

Rhythm is how we create the feeling of slowness. 

**Rules of Rhythm:**
- **Target Length:** 12–18 words per average sentence.
- **Maximum Length:** 25 words. If a sentence exceeds this, break it into two.
- **Paragraph Density:** 2–4 sentences per paragraph. Never present a wall of text. White space is essential.
- **Opening and Closing:** Never open a paragraph with a question. Never close a piece of writing with a question. Statements convey quiet confidence; questions often feel like marketing tactics.
- **Punctuation Constraints:** Never use exclamation marks. Use the em dash (with spaces on either side) for gentle pauses. Ensure the Oxford comma is always used.

## 7. Vocabulary Principles

Our vocabulary is highly controlled. (For exhaustive lists, consult [03-HOP-Language-Audit.md](file:///e:/HOP/docs/editorial/03-HOP-Language-Audit.md) and the `04-vocabulary-system/` directory).

- **Principle 1: Sensory over Abstract.** We do not say "beautiful." We say "woven with a slight friction." 
- **Principle 2: Naming.** We name the weaver, the technique, the motif, and the region. Specificity is the currency of trust.
- **Principle 3: Elimination of Commerce-Speak.** We do not use words that belong in a mall or a fast-fashion site. There is no "cart," only a "bag." There is no "shopping," only "exploring" or "discovering."

---

## 8. Reading Level

Our writing must be universally accessible without being simplistic. 

- **Target Grade Level:** 8th to 10th grade. 
- **Why:** We want our sensory descriptions to be immediately felt, not intellectually decoded. We use precise vocabulary (e.g., "selvedge," "warp"), but we place those words in simple, declarative sentences. 

---

## 9. Brand Values in Voice

Our five brand values must be evident in the way we write:

1. **Slowness:** Achieved through varied sentence rhythm, ample paragraph spacing, and descriptions that linger on the making process.
2. **Quietness:** Achieved by avoiding exclamation marks, urgency language, and superlatives. 
3. **Continuance:** Achieved by speaking about the garment's future, referencing heirlooms, aging, and generational wear.
4. **Authenticity:** Achieved by stating facts. Naming the weaver. Explaining the origin of the dye. Showing the flaw in the handloom.
5. **Intention:** Achieved through ruthless editing. Every word is deliberate.

---

## 10. Writing Principles

These six principles are the filter through which all copy must pass:

1. **Show, Don't Tell:** Do not say the saree is high quality. Describe the 120-count cotton and the three weeks it took to weave.
2. **Specific Over Generic:** Do not say "made in India." Say "woven by Master Weaver Raman in Kanchipuram." 
3. **Rooted in the Real:** Every claim must be traceable to a physical reality.
4. **Restrained:** Remove everything that does not serve the cloth or the story. 
5. **Human-Scale:** Tell stories of individual women and individual weavers, not broad demographics. 
6. **Trust Through Transparency:** Explain how to care for it. Admit if it will bleed colour. Hide nothing.

---

## 11. Brand Dos

- **DO** use sentence case for headlines.
- **DO** use curly quotes (" ") and apostrophes.
- **DO** use Indian English spelling (colour, honour, centre).
- **DO** refer to the House in the first-person plural (We/Our/Us).
- **DO** address the reader directly (You/Your).
- **DO** refer to the wearer in the third person (She/Her) when describing the experience of the garment.
- **DO** name the weaver whenever possible.

---

## 12. Brand Don'ts

- **DON'T** ever use exclamation marks.
- **DON'T** use urgency or FOMO tactics (Hurry, Limited Edition, Buy Now).
- **DON'T** use discount language (Sale, Offer, Deal). We never discount.
- **DON'T** use marketing superlatives (Luxury, Premium, Stunning, Gorgeous).
- **DON'T** use empowerment clichés (Empower, Celebrate).
- **DON'T** use "I", "one", or "they" as pronouns for the brand. 
- **DON'T** use "sari" — always use "saree".

## 13. Examples: The Voice in Action

### Product Description
**Generic (Don't):** 
This stunning premium silk sari is a must-have! Beautifully crafted with gold zari, it's perfect for your next big event. Shop now before it's gone!

**HOP Voice (Do):**
Woven over twenty days by Master Weaver Selvam in Kanchipuram. The weight of the pure mulberry silk settles instantly, offering a quiet drape that remembers its folds. The borders carry real silver zari, intended to soften into a gentle patina over the decades. 

### Call to Action (Button)
**Generic (Don't):** Buy Now / Add to Cart / Get Yours
**HOP Voice (Do):** Add to bag / Explore the collection / Discover the craft

### Email Welcome
**Generic (Don't):** Welcome to our brand! We're so excited to have you. Enjoy 10% off your first purchase!
**HOP Voice (Do):** Welcome to the House. We are honored you are here. In this space, we move slowly, taking time to understand the cloth, the hands that made it, and the quiet ritual of the drape.

## 14. Edge Cases: Difficult Conversations

How we handle friction defines our warmth. 

- **Complaints & Errors:** We take immediate, unreserved responsibility. We do not use passive voice to deflect blame (e.g., "mistakes were made"). We say, "We made an error, and we are sorry." We resolve the issue quietly and generously.
- **Out of Stock:** We do not apologize for scarcity, as scarcity is the natural result of handweaving. We explain the reality. "This piece has found its home. As it is woven by hand, we cannot rush another. We invite you to explore our current collections, or write to us to discuss a commission."
- **Delays:** We inform the woman immediately, anchoring the delay in the reality of the craft. "The monsoon rains have delayed the natural dyeing process for your saree. We refuse to compromise the depth of the indigo by rushing. We anticipate it will be ready to travel to you in two weeks."
- **Returns:** We process them with grace. We do not make her jump through hoops. "We understand this piece was not meant for you. Please enclose it in its original cotton muslin, and we will arrange for its safe return."
