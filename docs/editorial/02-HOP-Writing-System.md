## 1. Headline System

### Headline Philosophy
Headlines are the entry points to the House. They must be quiet, confident, and rooted in the cloth. We never shout, we never rush. A headline should feel like a deep breath.

### Headline Types & Character Limits
*   **Hero Headlines (Homepage, Collection Landings):** 30–50 characters. Establishes the mood and intention.
*   **Section Headlines (About, Process, Care):** 20–40 characters. Clear, orienting, and calm.
*   **Collection Headlines (Individual Collection Pages):** 15–30 characters. Usually the name of the collection and a brief modifier.
*   **Product Headlines (Product Detail Pages):** 20–40 characters. The specific name of the saree and its origin.
*   **Journal Headlines (Editorial Articles):** 40–60 characters. Tells a quiet story.
*   **Navigation Headlines (Menus):** 10–20 characters. Unambiguous signposts.

### Capitalization Rules
*   **Sentence Case:** All headlines use sentence case. (e.g., *The weight of woven gold*)
    *   *Reason:* Sentence case is conversational, unpretentious, and easier to read.
*   **Exceptions:** Proper nouns (House of Padmavati, Kanchipuram, Kalyani) and collection names are capitalized.

### Punctuation in Headlines
*   No periods at the end of headlines.
*   No exclamation marks, ever.
    *   *Reason:* Exclamation marks generate artificial noise and urgency.

### Headline Formulas and Patterns
*   **The Element + The Intention:** *Thread and time*, *Silk and memory*
*   **The Action + The Object:** *Draping the quiet hours*, *Honouring the loom*
*   **The Origin + The Craft:** *Woven in Varanasi*, *The hands of Kanchipuram*

### Examples
*   **Hero:** *A return to quiet making*
*   **Section:** *The hands behind the loom*
*   **Collection:** *The Kalyani collection*
*   **Product:** *Meenakshi in coastal teal*
*   **Journal:** *The memory carried in a single thread*
*   **Navigation:** *Our philosophy*

## 2. Paragraph System

### Paragraph Length Rules
*   **Length:** 2–4 sentences per paragraph. Max 25 words per sentence; average 12–18.
    *   *Reason:* Short paragraphs provide space and breath. They prevent the reader from feeling overwhelmed, embodying our value of "slowness."

### Paragraph Rhythm and Flow
*   Vary sentence lengths within the paragraph. Follow a short sentence with a longer, flowing one.
    *   *Reason:* This creates a cadence reminiscent of the rhythmic clack of the loom.

### Opening Paragraph Conventions
*   Start with the concrete, not the abstract. Begin with a sensory detail (the sound of silk, the weight of the zari).
    *   *Reason:* We show, we do not tell. Grounding the reader in reality builds immediate trust.

### Body Paragraph Conventions
*   Deliver the core information or story progression here. Maintain the focus on specific, real details (the weaver, the technique, the dye).

### Closing Paragraph Conventions
*   End on a lingering thought or a gentle invitation. Never end with a question.
    *   *Reason:* Questions demand immediate answers and create unrest. We offer quiet resolution.

### Transition Between Paragraphs
*   Transitions should rely on thematic progression, not mechanical connectors (avoid "Therefore," "Furthermore"). Let the thought carry over naturally.

## 3. CTA System

### CTA Philosophy
We suggest, invite, and welcome. We never command, rush, or push. The CTA is a gently opened door, not a flashing sign.

### Primary CTAs by Page Type
*   **Homepage:** *Explore the collection*, *Read our philosophy*
*   **Product Page:** *Add to bag* (Never "Buy now")
*   **Journal Page:** *Read the story*, *Return to journal*

### Secondary CTA Rules
*   Secondary CTAs should always be a step down in visual weight (text link or ghost button) and tonal force.
*   *Examples:* *View size guide*, *Learn about the weave*

### CTA Language Patterns
*   **Approved:** Explore, View, Discover, Read, Add to bag, Return.
*   **Forbidden:** Shop, Buy, Hurry, Get, Claim, Grab, Checkout now.

### CTA Placement Principles
*   Give CTAs breathing room. Do not cluster multiple actions together.
    *   *Reason:* "Jasmine Mist" (space and breath) applies to our UI structure as much as our philosophy.

### Examples with Rationale
*   *Explore the weaves* (Instead of "Shop all"): "Explore" invites a leisurely, self-directed journey; "Shop" is transactional.
*   *Add to bag* (Instead of "Add to cart"): A "bag" is personal and physical; a "cart" is industrial and generic.

## 4. Product Naming

### Naming Conventions for Individual Sarees
*   Every saree is named after a real or historically significant Indian woman, or a word denoting a specific grace or state of being.
*   Never use descriptive marketing labels (e.g., "The Premium Red Silk Saree").

### Name Format and Structure
*   **Format:** [Name] in [Colour/Motif]
*   **Example:** *Anjali in golden sand*, *Revathi in twilight indigo*

### What Product Names Should Evoke
*   Names should evoke respect, history, and human scale. They reinforce that the cloth is meant for a woman, not just a consumer.

## 5. Collection Naming

### Why Collections are Named After Women
*   Collections are named after women (Kalyani, Viara, Arya, Padma, Spandana) to honour the primary audience and the lineage of wearers. It brings the brand to a human scale.

### Naming Rules for Future Collections
*   Names must be Indian in origin.
*   They should ideally have a soft, resonant phonetic quality.
*   Avoid names that have become overly commercialized.

### Collection Tagline Conventions
*   A 3–5 word sentence case tagline follows the collection name, describing its essence.
*   *Example:* *The Kalyani collection — Woven for the quiet hours*

## 6. Button Writing

### Button Text Conventions
*   Keep it to 2–3 words.
*   Start with a gentle verb.
*   Sentence case only.

### Approved Button Labels with Rationale
*   *Add to bag:* Personal, non-urgent.
*   *Begin checkout:* Suggests the start of a process, rather than a forced finality.
*   *Submit order:* Clear, definitive, but respectful.
*   *Join our journal:* "Journal" implies thoughtful reading; "Newsletter" implies marketing noise.

### Max Character Count
*   Maximum 20 characters to maintain visual restraint.

### Tone in Buttons
*   Clear, polite, and completely devoid of urgency.

---

## 7. Navigation Labels

### All Navigation Label Conventions
*   One or two words maximum.
*   Sentence case.
*   No cleverness; prioritize absolute clarity.

### Approved Navigation Labels
*   *Collections*, *The House*, *Journal*, *Client care*, *Bag*

### Label Tone and Voice
*   Objective, orienting, and welcoming. Like a soft signpost in a quiet corridor.

---

## 8. Empty States

### Philosophy for Empty States
*   An empty state is not an error; it is a space waiting to be filled. The copy should feel like a gentle, well-lit room, not a dead end.

### Examples
*   **Empty Bag:** *Your bag is empty. Take your time exploring the collections.*
    *   *Reason:* Removes pressure, encourages slow browsing.
*   **Empty Wishlist:** *Your saved pieces will appear here.*
*   **Empty Orders:** *You have no past orders with the House.*
*   **No Search Results:** *We could not find anything for "[Term]". Perhaps explore our current collections.*

---

## 9. Error Messages

### Error Message Philosophy
*   Take the blame. Be warm, clear, and helpful. Never make the user feel at fault. Remove all technical jargon.

### Examples
*   **Form Errors:** *Please provide a valid email address so we may reach you.*
*   **Payment Errors:** *We were unable to process this payment. Please verify your details or try a different method.*
*   **System Errors:** *Our systems are resting for a moment. Please try again shortly.*
*   **404:** *This page has been moved or no longer exists. Let us guide you back to the collections.*

---

## 10. Forms

### Form Label Conventions
*   Sentence case. Place labels above fields for readability.
*   *Example:* *Email address*, *Delivery location*

### Placeholder Text Rules
*   Do not use placeholder text as a substitute for labels. Use it only for formatting examples.
*   *Example:* *name@domain.com*

### Help Text Conventions
*   Brief, explanatory, placed below the field.
*   *Example:* *We use this only to send you updates regarding your order.*

### Validation Message Conventions
*   Appear inline, clearly indicating what is needed without using red, alarming text or exclamation marks.
*   *Example:* *This field requires a response.*

---

## 11. Captions

### Image Caption Conventions
*   Focus on the detail being shown. Do not describe what is obvious; describe the invisible effort.
*   *Example:* *The zari border on the Padma saree, hand-twisted over three days.*

### Video Caption Conventions
*   Brief context setting.
*   *Example:* *Weaver Narayan at the loom in Varanasi.*

### Gallery Caption Conventions
*   Keep it to one sentence. Identify the weave, the location, or the artisan.

---

## 12. Microcopy

### Tooltip Conventions
*   Used sparingly, only to define a specific weaving term or clarify a process. Max one sentence.

### Loading States
*   Avoid witty loading copy. Use a simple, reassuring phrase.
*   *Example:* *Preparing your view...*

### Success Messages
*   Quiet confirmation.
*   *Example:* *Your order has been received by the House.*

### Confirmation Dialogs
*   Ask clearly, provide clear options.
*   *Example:* *Remove this item from your bag?* [Remove] / [Keep item]

### Toast Notifications
*   Transient and non-intrusive.
*   *Example:* *Added to your wishlist.*

---

## 13. Accessibility Writing

### Alt Text Conventions
*   Describe the image functionally for someone who cannot see it, maintaining the brand's quiet dignity.
*   *Example:* *A close-up of a teal silk saree, showing the gold zari pattern woven into the pallu.*

### ARIA Label Conventions
*   Use standard, expected terms for screen readers, overriding stylized visual text if necessary for clarity.
*   *Example:* Visual: *Bag*, ARIA: *Shopping bag with 2 items*.

### Screen Reader Considerations
*   Ensure logical reading order. Do not rely on visual cues (like "click the button on the right").

### Focus State Text
*   Ensure text remains legible and clear when focused; rely on the OS defaults or subtle visual shifts that don't alter the copy.

---

## 14. UX Writing Principles

### Clarity Over Cleverness
*   We never sacrifice understanding for a poetic turn of phrase in our UI. The poetry belongs in the journal; the UI must guide the user safely.

### Guidance Over Commands
*   We lead the user, we do not dictate to them. (e.g., "Please sign in" rather than "Login required").

### Consistency Over Variety
*   Call the same thing by the same name everywhere. A "bag" is always a "bag," never a "cart" or a "basket."

### Warmth in Functional Moments
*   The transaction is where the brand is most vulnerable to becoming generic. We inject our warmth here (e.g., "Your parcel will be prepared with care").
