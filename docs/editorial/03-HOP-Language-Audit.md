# HOP Language Audit

**Status:** Production
**Last Updated:** July 2026

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document serves as the definitive language blacklist and substitution guide for all House of Padmavati (HOP) content. The words we choose not to say are as vital as the ones we do. Every omission serves the House's broader vision of quietness, substance, and intention.

**Why this audit exists:**
To protect the HOP voice from industry clichés, artificial urgency, and generic praise. We aim for human-scale communication, relying on specific truths rather than vague modifiers. 

**How to use it:**
Consult this document during the editing phase of any content creation. Use the substitution tables to replace forbidden terms with sensory, specific, and grounded alternatives. 

**When to consult it:**
- When drafting or reviewing product descriptions
- When writing journal entries, emails, or social copy
- When configuring automated messaging or system responses

*Cross-reference:* [Voice Bible](file:///e:/HOP/docs/editorial/01-HOP-Voice-Bible.md), [Forbidden Words](file:///e:/HOP/docs/04-vocabulary-system/forbidden-words.md), [Approved Words](file:///e:/HOP/docs/04-vocabulary-system/approved-words.md).

## 2. Words Never Used

These words dilute our message, cheapen the craft, or belong to different brand territories.

### a. Luxury Clichés
Words that insist on their own value rather than demonstrating it.
- **Luxury / Premium:** Tries too hard. *Alternative: Refined, considered, House.*
- **Exquisite / Opulent / Lavish:** Overwrought and noisy. *Alternative: Intricate, dense, layered.*
- **Glamorous / Extravagant:** Suggests excess and performance. *Alternative: Striking, deliberate.*
- **Plush / Sumptuous:** Belongs to upholstery, not weaving. *Alternative: Weighted, soft, substantial.*

### b. Generic Praise
Adjectives that do no actual descriptive work.
- **Beautiful / Stunning / Gorgeous:** Subjective and empty. *Alternative: (Describe the specific physical trait—e.g., iridescent, deeply saturated).*
- **Lovely / Amazing / Incredible:** Conversational filler. *Alternative: Exceptional, rare.*
- **Wonderful / Fabulous:** Belongs to casual speech, not the House. *Alternative: Distinct, memorable.*

### c. Urgency and Scarcity
Tactics that disrespect the woman's agency and the slowness of the craft.
- **Hurry / Last chance / Don't miss out:** Manufactured panic. *Alternative: (Silence).*
- **Limited edition / Only X left:** Treats scarcity as a gimmick. *Alternative: Woven in a single run, few remain.*
- **While stocks last / One-time / Limited time:** Transactional pressure. *Alternative: Available until spoken for.*
- **Exclusive:** Suggests exclusion for status. *Alternative: Reserved for, dedicated to.*

### d. Marketing and Ecommerce Language
Terms that break the fourth wall of the experience.
- **Shop now / Buy now:** Aggressive commands. *Alternative: Browse the collection, acquire.*
- **Sale / Discount / Offer / Deal:** The House does not discount. *Alternative: (Silence).*
- **Free shipping:** Focuses on logistics over the object. *Alternative: Compliments of the House.*
- **Checkout / Add to cart:** Purely functional. *Alternative: Secure this piece, Add to bag.*
- **Subscribe / Sign up:** Transactional exchange. *Alternative: Receive letters, Join the House.*

### e. Fashion Industry Clichés
Language that ties the garment to passing trends rather than generational permanence.
- **Wardrobe essential / Must-have / It-saree:** Prescriptive and generic. *Alternative: A foundational drape, a cornerstone.*
- **Statement piece / Elevate your style:** Implies the woman needs fixing. *Alternative: A distinctive weave, a bold choice.*
- **Investment piece:** Reduces art to financial return. *Alternative: An heirloom, meant for the next generation.*
- **Transitional piece / Capsule wardrobe:** Editorial filler. *Alternative: Adaptable, enduring.*
- **Fashion-forward / Trend / Trending:** The House exists outside of seasons. *Alternative: Timeless, grounded.*

### f. Empty Intensifiers
Adverbs that weaken the word they modify.
- **Absolutely / Truly / Really:** Unnecessary padding. *Alternative: (Remove).*
- **Very / Extremely / Incredibly:** Vague magnitudes. *Alternative: (Find a stronger root verb or noun).*
- **Perfect / Ultimate / Best:** Impossible absolutes. *Alternative: Ideal, defining, unparalleled.*
- **Pure:** Allowed *only* when referring to certified materials (e.g., pure silk, pure silver zari). Never as an abstract modifier (e.g., "pure elegance").

### g. Abstract Branding
Corporate phrasing masquerading as empathy.
- **Lifestyle / Experience (marketing use):** Vague and overused. *Alternative: Way of living, ritual.*
- **Journey (marketing use):** Cliché storytelling. *Alternative: Process, origin.*
- **Community (pushed):** Communities form organically; they are not marketed. *Alternative: Patrons, friends of the House.*
- **Ecosystem / Solution:** Tech jargon. *Alternative: The collection, the House.*
- **Empower / Celebrate:** Patronizing assumptions about the wearer. *Alternative: Honour, recognise.*

## 3. Phrases Never Used

Phrases that rely on tropes rather than specific, grounded storytelling.

- **"Elevate your wardrobe":** Implies inadequacy. *Alternative: Bring depth to your collection.*
- **"Treat yourself" / "You deserve this":** Cheapens the purchase to an impulse buy. *Alternative: (Focus on the object, not the psychology of the purchase).*
- **"Curated for you" / "Handpicked selection":** Overused internet clichés. *Alternative: Gathered, chosen.*
- **"The perfect gift":** Presumptuous. *Alternative: A meaningful offering.*
- **"A touch of elegance":** Vague and hollow. *Alternative: (Describe the exact detail, e.g., A border woven in silver).*
- **"Timeless beauty":** A cliché that means nothing. *Alternative: Designed for permanence.*
- **"Redefining luxury":** Grandiose and generic. *Alternative: Rooted in quietness and craft.*

## 4. AI Clichés

Language patterns commonly generated by large language models that feel synthetic and lack humanity.

- **"In today's world" / "In a fast-paced world":** Sweeping, unnecessary context. *Alternative: (Delete entirely and start with the specific subject).*
- **"It's important to note that":** Filler that distances the reader. *Alternative: (Just state the fact).*
- **"At the end of the day":** Conversational cliché. *Alternative: Ultimately, finally.*
- **"This is a testament to":** Melodramatic transition. *Alternative: This demonstrates, this shows.*
- **"Seamlessly blends":** The most common AI transition phrase. *Alternative: Combines, brings together.*
- **"A symphony of":** Overused metaphor for complexity. *Alternative: A complex weave, a dense pattern.*

## 5. Corporate Jargon

Terms that belong in boardrooms, not in the House.

- **Leverage:** Use.
- **Synergy:** Harmony, balance.
- **Holistic:** Complete, whole.
- **Actionable:** Practical, clear.
- **Scalable:** (Never applicable to handloom).
- **Paradigm:** Model, approach.
- **Impactful:** Meaningful, resonant.

## 6. Overused Editorial Phrases

Phrases that sound like magazine filler from a bygone era.

- **"In a world where..."** Cinematic cliché. *Alternative: State the House's position directly.*
- **"More than just a..."** A defensive structure. *Alternative: (Define exactly what it is).*
- **"At the intersection of..."** Overused conceptual framework. *Alternative: Where [A] meets [B].*

## 8. Technical Terms Policy

The House differentiates between System 1 (narrative, emotional) and System 2 (technical, factual) copy.

- **System 1 (The Story):** Forbidden technical jargon. Focus on weight, drape, and feel.
- **System 2 (The Details/Accordions):** Technical terms are permitted and encouraged for transparency.
  - Allowed in System 2: *Momme, GSM, SKU, Warp, Weft, Thread count, Micron.*
  - Forbidden in System 1: *Momme, GSM, SKU.*

## 9. Contextual Exceptions

A forbidden word may occasionally appear in HOP content under strict conditions:
- **Direct Quotes:** If a named artisan or weaver uses a word, we transcribe it faithfully (e.g., if a weaver says "beautiful," we retain it in their quote).
- **Policy/Legal Language:** Terms like "shipping," "checkout," and "transaction" are permitted in formal terms of service or privacy policies where legal clarity supersedes editorial voice.

## 10. Language Audit Checklist

Before publishing, ask:
- [ ] Is there any word attempting to create false urgency?
- [ ] Are adjectives specific and sensory, rather than generic praise?
- [ ] Have all corporate, AI, and fashion industry clichés been removed?
- [ ] Is the action requested (e.g., "Add to bag") polite and inviting?
- [ ] Are technical details appropriately placed in System 2 copy?
- [ ] Does every sentence sound like a human speaking quietly to another human?
