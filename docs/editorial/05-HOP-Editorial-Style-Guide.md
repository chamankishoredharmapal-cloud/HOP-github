# House of Padmavati: Editorial Style Guide

This document is the comprehensive style reference for all House of Padmavati (HOP) editorial content. It consolidates and extends our baseline grammatical rules to provide complete guidance for writers, editors, and digital creators.

For foundational vocabulary and forbidden words, please refer to [Vocabulary System](../04-vocabulary-system/).

## 1. Grammar

Our voice is warm, reserved, and precise. We use grammar not to constrain, but to clarify and elevate our storytelling.

- **Active vs. Passive Voice:** Prefer the active voice. It is clearer, more direct, and places agency where it belongs. *Correct:* "The master weaver tied the knots." *Incorrect:* "The knots were tied by the master weaver." Use passive voice only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- **Sentence Construction:** Avoid weak openings like "There is" or "There are." Ensure every sentence has a strong, active verb. Avoid dangling prepositions.
- **That vs. Which:** Use "that" for essential clauses (without commas). Use "which" for non-essential clauses (with commas).
- **Who vs. That:** Use "who" when referring to people (e.g., weavers, wearers). Use "that" when referring to objects or the House.
- **Less vs. Fewer:** Use "fewer" for countable items (fewer sarees, fewer threads). Use "less" for uncountable concepts (less time, less weight).
- **Among vs. Between:** Use "between" for two entities. Use "among" for three or more.
- **Since vs. Because:** Use "because" for cause and effect. Use "since" when referring to time.
- **Contractions:** Contractions (we're, you'll, it's) are permitted. We are a warm House, not a stiff institution. However, avoid overly casual or obscure contractions.
- **Negatives:** Avoid double negatives. Prefer positive constructions for clarity. *Correct:* "We choose natural dyes." *Incorrect:* "We do not use unnatural dyes."

## 2. Capitalisation

- **Headlines:** Always use sentence case for headlines, page titles, and section headings. Do not use title case.
- **Brand Name:** Always write as "House of Padmavati" with a capital H and P. The acronym "HOP" is strictly for internal documentation and should never appear in public-facing copy.
- **Collection Names:** Use Title Case for collection names (e.g., The Kalyani Collection, The Viara Collection).
- **Product Names:** Use Sentence case for specific product descriptions or titles unless referring to a named collection piece.
- **Job Titles:** Lowercase job titles (master weaver, creative director) unless they immediately precede a name as a formal title.
- **Navigation Labels:** Use Sentence case for all navigation and UI labels.

## 3. Numbers

- **General Rule:** Spell out numbers one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above.
- **Large Numbers:** Use commas for numbers with four or more digits (e.g., 1,200; 24,000).
- **Narrative vs. Functional:** In narrative text, spell out specific craft numbers for rhythm and weight (e.g., "sixty hours of weaving," "twenty-one days"). In technical specifications and functional areas, use numerals (e.g., "60 hours", "21 days").

## 4. Dates

- **Format:** Write dates as Day Month Year without commas or ordinals.
  - *Correct:* 12 July 2026
  - *Incorrect:* July 12, 2026; 12th July 2026

## 5. Time

- **Format:** Use lowercase letters with periods for a.m. and p.m.
  - *Correct:* 5:47 a.m.
  - *Incorrect:* 5:47 AM, 5:47 am

## 6. Measurements

- **Format:** Use numerals for all measurements. Spell out the unit in narrative text, but abbreviate in functional or specification contexts.
  - *Correct (Narrative):* 5.5 metres, 12 momme
  - *Correct (Functional):* 5.5m

## 7. Currency

- **Format:** Always include a space after the rupee symbol. Use Indian number formatting (commas after the thousand, lakh, crore).
  - *Correct:* ₹ 24,000
- **Context:** Never frame the price in narrative text. Price is a functional detail, not a selling point or a matter for discussion.

## 8. Typography References

- **Heading Hierarchy:** Use a logical, sequential hierarchy (H1, H2, H3) without skipping levels.
- **Body Text:** Maintain a readable measure (line length). Paragraphs should be 2–4 sentences.
- **Small Text:** Legal text, footnotes, and tertiary specifications may use small text, but must maintain adequate contrast and readability.
- **Pull Quotes:** Use pull quotes to highlight foundational thoughts or weaver testimonials, separated from body text with ample white space.

## 9. Punctuation

- **Oxford Comma:** Required in all lists of three or more items.
- **Em Dashes:** Use em dashes (—) for parenthetical emphasis or a break in thought. Always include a single space on both sides.
- **En Dashes:** Use en dashes (–) without spaces for ranges (e.g., 2024–2026, pages 12–14).
- **Hyphens:** Use for compound modifiers preceding a noun (e.g., hand-spun yarn) but not when following a verb.
- **Quotation Marks:** Always use curly, typographic quotes (“ ”).
- **Apostrophes:** Always use curly, typographic apostrophes (’), not straight, typewriter marks.
- **Colons:** Use to introduce lists or a definitive statement. Capitalise the first word after a colon only if it begins a complete sentence.
- **Semicolons:** Use sparingly, typically to join closely related independent clauses when a period feels too abrupt.
- **Ellipses:** Use rarely. When used, insert spaces between the dots (. . .).
- **Lists (Bullet Points):** See Section 11.
- **Never Use:**
  - Exclamation marks
  - ALL-CAPS text (except in code or specified UI elements)
  - Ampersands (&) in body copy (always spell out "and")
  - Double spaces after periods
  - Straight quotes or straight apostrophes
  - Emojis of any kind

## 10. Formatting

- **Bold:** Use sparingly. Reserve for emphasis in internal documentation only. Do not bold text in external narrative copy.
- **Italics:** Use for publication titles, collection names (when appropriate for distinction), foreign words on their first use, and gentle emphasis.
- **Links:** Link text must be descriptive. Never use bare URLs or generic text like "click here." *Correct:* "Read our return policy." *Incorrect:* "Click here for returns."

## 11. Lists

- Capitalise the first word of every bullet point.
- If a bullet point is a complete sentence, end it with a period.
- If a bullet point is a sentence fragment, use no terminal punctuation.
- Ensure all items in a list are syntactically parallel and grammatically consistent. Do not mix complete sentences and fragments in the same list.

## 12. Quotes

- **Formatting:** Always use curly quotes.
- **Nested Quotes:** Use single quotes for quotes within quotes (“The master weaver said, ‘This motif is older than my father,’ and smiled.”).
- **Attribution:** Place attributions clearly, usually following the first complete clause or at the end of the quote.

## 13. Emphasis

- **Restraint Principle:** Rely on the meaning of words, not typographical tricks, to convey emotion. Choose one precise, powerful adjective over two generic ones.
- **Em Dash:** Use the spaced em dash ( — ) to gently set apart a thought or add parenthetical emphasis.
- **Rhythm:** Create emphasis by placing a short, decisive sentence immediately following a longer, flowing one.

## 14. Product Titles

- **Format and Structure:** Keep titles descriptive, factual, and dignified.
- **Inclusions:** Include the primary colour or weave type and the defining motif. (e.g., Kanjeevaram with Lotus Motif).
- **Omissions:** Never include marketing adjectives (Beautiful, Stunning), generic terms (Sari, Saree, unless required for clarity), or urgency markers.

## 15. Collection Names

- **Capitalisation:** Use Title Case (The Kalyani Collection).
- **Running Text:** When referenced in narrative copy, treat the collection name with respect but avoid making it overly promotional. “In the Kalyani collection, we explore the depth of madder red.”

## 16. Accessibility

- **Alt Text:** Write descriptive, functional alt text for all images. Describe what is happening in the image, not what it represents conceptually. Never use decorative alt text.
- **ARIA Labels:** Ensure ARIA labels are clear and functional for screen readers.
- **Reading Order:** Structure documents and pages so the linear reading order makes logical sense.
- **Colour Contrast:** Ensure all text, especially small text, meets WCAG AA contrast ratios at a minimum.
- **Plain Language:** Use clear, unambiguous language in all functional text (buttons, error messages, navigation).

## 17. SEO Considerations

- **Title Tags:** Format as: [Page Title] | House of Padmavati. Keep under 60 characters.
- **Meta Descriptions:** Provide an accurate, engaging summary of the page using brand vocabulary. Keep under 155 characters. No urgency or clickbait.
- **Heading Hierarchy:** Use H1, H2, and H3 logically to outline the page content for crawlers.
- **Alt Text:** Use descriptive alt text that naturally incorporates relevant, brand-approved keywords without stuffing.
- **URLs:** Keep URLs short, lowercase, and hyphen-separated. Avoid dynamic parameters where possible.

## 18. Consistency Rules

- **Spelling:** Always use Indian English spelling (e.g., colour, centre, honour, lustre).
- **Terminology:** Always use "saree", not "sari". Refer to the [Vocabulary System](../04-vocabulary-system/) for preferred terms like *handwoven*, *zari*, and *pallu*.
- **Abbreviations:** Avoid abbreviations unless universally understood (e.g., UI, UX). When in doubt, spell it out.
- **Cross-Documentation:** Before defining a new rule, ensure it does not contradict the Voice Bible or existing editorial guidelines. If an update is required, update all related documents.
