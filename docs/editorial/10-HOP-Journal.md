# The HOP Journal

This document defines the editorial philosophy, content model, and quality standards for the HOP Journal. It must be read in conjunction with [01-HOP-Voice-Bible.md](01-HOP-Voice-Bible.md) and [04-HOP-Content-Architecture.md](04-HOP-Content-Architecture.md).

## 1. Editorial Philosophy

The Journal exists to deepen the reader's relationship with craft, culture, and the house. It is not a content marketing channel. We do not publish to fill a calendar; we publish only when there is something to say. 

Articles in the Journal are gifts to the reader, regardless of their purchase intent. They require no transaction to yield value. The Journal is the intellectual and emotional centre of the digital fashion house—it is what separates House of Padmavati from an online store. 

## 2. Article Structure

Every article follows a considered structure to ensure a calm, distraction-free reading space.

### Required Blocks
- **Hero Image:** A single, horizontal image that establishes mood or context. 
- **Title:** Sentence case, evocative.
- **Subtitle:** One sentence providing context or a secondary thought.
- **Author:** Usually "House of Padmavati" unless a specific guest author is credited.
- **Date:** Spelled out (e.g., 14 July 2026).
- **Body:** The primary text, structured for slowness.
- **Closure:** A quiet end note, never a hard call to action.

### Optional Blocks
- **Pull Quotes:** Used sparingly to emphasize a core truth.
- **Image Galleries:** Two to three related images, evenly spaced.
- **Captions:** Providing specific, factual context for images.
- **Related Articles:** Two thoughtfully chosen suggestions at the page's end.

## 3. Titles

Titles should invite contemplation. They are evocative rather than purely descriptive, though they must never obscure the subject. 

- **Casing:** Sentence case only.
- **Length:** 50–80 characters. 

### Title Formulas
- *The Material Focus:* Naming the specific element (e.g., The weight of unbleached cotton)
- *The Process Observation:* Naming the action (e.g., Counting threads in Kanchipuram)
- *The Cultural Tie:* Connecting cloth to context (e.g., Jasmine borders and evening rituals)

### Examples
**Approved Titles:**
- *The quiet geometry of a Gadwal border* (Focuses on specific craft detail)
- *Waiting for the monsoon in Maheshwar* (Locates the craft in time and place)
- *How we fold the silk* (Simple, intimate, specific)

**Rejected Titles:**
- *5 Reasons to Love Gadwal Sarees* (Listicle format, marketing tone)
- *Stunning Kanchipuram Silks You Must Have!* (Forbidden words, exclamation mark, urgency)
- *The History of Handloom Weaving* (Too generic, sounds like a textbook)

## 4. Introductions

The opening paragraph sets the pace. We do not start with grand declarations, questions, or brand statements. We start in the middle of a moment.

- Begin with a sensory detail, a specific observation, or a quiet action.
- Never open with a rhetorical question.
- Keep to 2–3 sentences maximum.

### Examples
**Approved:**
The air in the weaving shed smells faintly of rice paste and damp earth. Here, the rhythm of the fly shuttle dictates the pace of the afternoon. 

**Rejected:**
Have you ever wondered how a handloom saree is made? At House of Padmavati, we celebrate the amazing journey of our weavers.

## 5. Body

The body text must sustain the quietness established in the introduction. 

- **Paragraph Length:** 2–4 sentences. One central idea per paragraph. 
- **Rhythm:** Alternate sentence lengths. Average 12–18 words, maximum 25 words. 
- **Sensory Language:** Show the reader the texture, the weight, the sound of the loom. Use specific nouns (weft, shuttle, zari, selvedge).
- **Transitions:** Move gently between paragraphs. Avoid mechanical transitions like "firstly" or "furthermore." 

### Word Count Guidelines
- **Field Notes:** 300–500 words
- **Craft Notes:** 600–800 words
- **House Letters:** 400–600 words
- **Weaver Portraits:** 800–1,200 words

## 6. Captions

Captions ground our imagery in reality. They are informative, specific, and never decorative. 

- **Format:** Sentence case, no terminal punctuation if it is a fragment, full stop if it is a complete sentence.
- **Tone:** Factual, naming the place, person, or technique.

### Examples
- *Approved:* Preparing the warp threads in Srikakulam.
- *Approved:* Master weaver Kamala adjusting the tension on a cotton and silk blend.
- *Rejected:* So gorgeous! Weaving magic on the loom.

## 7. Pull Quotes

Pull quotes offer a moment of pause. They should highlight a sentence that carries emotional weight or technical clarity.

- **When to use:** In articles exceeding 600 words, use one pull quote to break up text visually.
- **Selection:** Choose sentences that stand alone conceptually.
- **Formatting:** Displayed in a larger serif font, slightly indented, with em dashes separating the quote from the body text.

## 8. Metadata

Every Journal entry requires strict metadata for the content management system. 

- **Title:** Sentence case, 50–80 characters.
- **Slug:** lowercase-with-hyphens.
- **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD format for the system.
- **Author:** "House of Padmavati" or full name of guest.
- **Category:** Select from the six defined categories.
- **Tags:** Specific weave (e.g., chanderi), material (e.g., mulberry-silk), or region. Maximum 3 tags.
- **Excerpt:** 1–2 sentences summarizing the article's core thought. No marketing language.
- **SEO Title:** 50–60 characters, natural language.
- **SEO Description:** 150–160 characters. Informative, calm, incorporating specific keywords without stuffing.
- **Hero Image:** Absolute path to a high-resolution, uncropped image.
- **Hero Alt Text:** Literal description of the image for accessibility (e.g., A woman's hands tying cotton threads before dyeing).

## 9. Categories

The Journal contains six distinct categories. Every article must fit into one.

### a. Craft Notes
Deep dives into weaving techniques, materials, and processes.
- **Purpose:** To educate the reader on the specific labour and skill required.
- **Tone:** Knowledgeable, precise, technical but accessible.
- **Typical Length:** 600–800 words.
- **Example Topic:** The difference between a cut-work and a kadhwa border.

### b. Weaver Portraits
Profiles of individual weavers, their lives, and their work.
- **Purpose:** To name the maker and humanize the craft.
- **Tone:** Respectful, observational, centering the weaver, not the house.
- **Typical Length:** 800–1,200 words.
- **Example Topic:** A morning with Saraswati in her Maheshwar workshop.

### c. Field Notes
Observations from visits to weaving communities.
- **Purpose:** To share the atmosphere and reality of the origin points.
- **Tone:** Sensory, atmospheric, personal.
- **Typical Length:** 300–500 words.
- **Example Topic:** The sounds of the loom in early monsoon.

### d. House Letters
Reflections from the house on values, craft, and culture.
- **Purpose:** To articulate the philosophy of House of Padmavati.
- **Tone:** Warm, deliberate, philosophical.
- **Typical Length:** 400–600 words.
- **Example Topic:** Why we do not believe in discounts.

### e. Ritual Guides
Guides to wearing, caring for, and living with sarees.
- **Purpose:** To ensure the continuance of the cloth.
- **Tone:** Helpful, gentle, practical.
- **Typical Length:** 500–700 words.
- **Example Topic:** Storing silk in muslin and neem.

### f. Glossary
Definitions of craft terms, weave types, and regional techniques.
- **Purpose:** To provide a reference point for our specific vocabulary.
- **Tone:** Factual, concise.
- **Typical Length:** 100–200 words per entry.
- **Example Topic:** Defining the pallu.

## 10. Authoring Rules

- **Voice Consistency:** All articles, unless explicitly guest-authored, must sound like the house—warm, reserved, and precise.
- **Fact-checking Requirements:** Every claim about a weave, a material, or a historical technique must be traceable to a source. Accuracy is respect.
- **Image Requirements:** Only real photography commissioned by the house or archival images. Never stock photography.
- **Review and Approval:** Every piece must be reviewed for voice and factual accuracy before publication. 
- **Publication Frequency:** There is no quota. We publish only when a piece meets our standards and we have something meaningful to share.
- **Archival Policy:** Articles remain indefinitely. We do not remove old posts, as they form the living history of the house.

## 11. Journal Landing Page

The Journal index is a reading room, not a blog. 

- **Page Purpose:** To invite the reader to sit, slow down, and read. 
- **Layout:** A single featured article at the top, clear category filters, and a quiet grid of article cards below.
- **Emotional Objective:** Contemplation, curiosity. The page should feel spacious (60% Jasmine Mist).

## 12. SEO Strategy

Our approach to search engines is the same as our approach to the reader: we provide clear, truthful, well-structured information.

- **Approach:** Write for the reader first. Search engines reward specific, high-quality, deeply researched content.
- **Keyword Integration:** Natural and contextually appropriate. We will use terms like "handwoven Chanderi saree" because that is precisely what the subject is, not to satisfy an algorithm. 
- **Internal Linking:** We link gently between Journal articles and specific pieces in the collection only when relevant. If a Craft Note discusses a specific border technique, it may link to a piece in the Kalyani collection that features that border, without using aggressive anchor text.
