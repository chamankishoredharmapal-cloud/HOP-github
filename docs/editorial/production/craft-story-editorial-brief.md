# Editorial Brief: Craft Story (First 2)

## Strategic Mandate
- **Purpose:** Educate readers about specific weaving techniques (Zari and Pit Loom) to build HOP's authority as a cultural custodian.
- **Business Goal:** Build deep trust that justifies price points. Convert readers into informed buyers who understand the labour behind the product.
- **Brand Goal:** Show that HOP knows craft deeply, avoiding generic terms like "handwoven" in favour of precise technical vocabulary.

## Emotional Targeting
- **Primary Emotion:** Wonder (at the complexity and time required).
- **Secondary Emotion:** Trust (in HOP's knowledge).
- **Customer Journey Stage:** Wonder → Trust.
- **Emotional Arc:** The reader transitions from thinking of a saree as a product to understanding it as a system of decisions and human effort.

## Reader
- **Primary Persona:** The Woman Who Chooses Well, curious about how her clothes are made and what makes them last.
- **Reader Intent:** "What makes this saree special? How is it made?"
- **Trust Requirement:** Avoid romanticising the poverty or hardship of weavers. Focus on the precision, geometry, and mastery of the craft.

## Content Priorities
1. **Instruction Arc:** Clear progression from Hook → Technique → Process → Importance.
2. **Technical Depth:** Explain *how* things work (e.g., silver wire wrapping around a silk core; treadles in a pit).
3. **Longevity:** Explain how these techniques affect the fabric over time (e.g., real zari softening vs imitation stiffening).

## Constraints
- **Forbidden Words:** Luxury, Premium, Exquisite, Magic, Authentic.
- **Tone Register:** Formality 3, Warmth 3, Sensory 4, Technical 4, Urgency 0.
- **Format:** `type: craft-note` with blocks (hero, intro, body, image, pull-quote, system-2, closure).

## Production Scope
1. **Understanding Zari:** Review existing draft, ensure it aligns with the 12-pass standards, and output as clean.
2. **The Pit Loom:** Create new draft focused on the physical mechanics of the loom and the human rhythm it imparts to the cloth.
