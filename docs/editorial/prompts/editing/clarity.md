# Editing: Clarity

**Purpose:** Improve the clarity and readability of HOP content without changing its voice, sensory quality, or emotional resonance.

**When to use:** Content that is structurally sound but difficult to follow. Complex sentences, ambiguous references, or unclear logical flow.

**When NOT to use:** For proofreading (use `editing/proofread.md`). For voice transformation (use `editing/rewrite.md`). For luxury refinement (use `editing/luxury-tone.md`).

---

## Purpose

Make HOP content easier to read and understand. Clarity edits remove ambiguity, simplify complex constructions, and improve logical flow — all while preserving HOP's warm, sensory voice.

## Required Inputs

- The content to clarify
- (Optional) The aspects that are unclear: sentence length, logical flow, ambiguous references, or reading level

## Expected Outputs

- Clarified content
- A note on what was changed and why

## Constraints

- Preserve sensory density — clarity should not strip away descriptive language
- Preserve HOP's sentence rhythm — do not make every sentence the same length
- Preserve emotional resonance — clarity should make the feeling clearer, not remove it
- Do not increase reading level — if anything, lower it

## Clarity Targets

### 1. Long Sentences
Break sentences over 25 words into shorter ones.

**Before:** "The zari border, which has been hand-spun using traditional methods passed down through four generations of weavers in the Molakalmuru region of Karnataka, catches the morning light in a way that makes the saree feel alive."

**After:** "The zari border catches the morning light. It has been hand-spun the same way for four generations, in Molakalmuru, Karnataka. The light makes the saree feel alive."

### 2. Ambiguous References
Ensure pronouns and references are clear.

**Before:** "The weaver showed her daughter how to thread the loom. She said it takes patience." (Who is "she"? What is "it"?)

**After:** "Gangamma showed her daughter how to thread the loom. 'The work takes patience,' she said."

### 3. Passive Constructions
Convert unnecessary passive voice to active voice.

**Before:** "The tension is adjusted by the weaver twice every hour to ensure the weave remains even."

**After:** "The weaver adjusts the tension twice an hour."

### 4. Nominalizations
Replace noun-heavy constructions with verb-driven sentences.

**Before:** "The completion of the weaving process requires the application of consistent tension."

**After:** "The weave requires consistent tension."

### 5. Negatives
Replace negative constructions with positive ones.

**Before:** "The design is not uncommon in this region."

**After:** "The design is common in this region."

## Examples

**Before:**
"The saree, which is made from a combination of Mulberry silk and hand-spun zari that has been sourced from the weavers of Molakalmuru, represents the culmination of decades of traditional knowledge that has been passed down through multiple generations of skilled artisans who have dedicated their lives to preserving this ancient craft."

**After:**
"This saree is woven from Mulberry silk and hand-spun zari. The silk comes from Molakalmuru, where the knowledge has been passed down through generations. The weave carries decades of tradition."

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Removing warmth | Clarity does not mean cold. Keep HOP's warm, personal tone. |
| Making all sentences the same length | Vary rhythm. Three short, one long, repeat. |
| Over-simplifying | Clarity for a Grade 9 reader does not mean Grade 3. Maintain appropriate reading level. |
| Removing sensory language | Sensory density is part of HOP's voice. Clarify around sensory anchors, do not remove them. |
