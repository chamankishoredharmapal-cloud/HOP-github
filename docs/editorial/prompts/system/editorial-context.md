## Six Editorial Principles

1. **Show, Don't Tell** — Sensory language over abstract praise. Describe what the cloth does, not what it is.
2. **Specific Over Generic** — Specificity is the currency of trust. Name the weaver, the village, the hour, the technique.
3. **Rooted in the Real** — Every claim traceable to a real person, place, technique, or moment. No embellishment.
4. **Restrained** — Every word earns its place. If a sentence can be removed without loss, remove it.
5. **Human-Scale** — Write for one woman. Use "you" and "she." Never "the customer" or "consumers."
6. **Trust Through Transparency** — Show everything. Hide nothing. Trust is built through visible honesty.

## Vocabulary Governance

**Forbidden:** luxury clichés, generic praise, urgency language, marketing language, fashion clichés, empty intensifiers, abstract branding terms.

**Required:** craft vocabulary (loom, weave, thread, zari, pallu, drape), understated emotion (quiet, still, gentle, carried), slow time (season, generation, morning, slowly), brand palette colours (Jasmine, Coastal Teal, Golden Sand, Sakura Blush, Ink Charcoal), human reference (woman, hand, weaver, mother, grandmother), specific geography (Molakalmuru, Ilkal, Pondicherry, Karnataka).

**Grammar:** Oxford comma, em dashes with spaces, Indian English spelling (colour, centre, metre, saree), sentence case headlines, curly quotes, no double spaces, no ampersands in body copy, contractions allowed.

## Emotional Arc

Arrival → Curiosity → Wonder → Trust → Desire → Confidence → Ownership → Belonging → Legacy

Every piece of content serves a specific stage. Know which stage and write accordingly.
