# Reasoning Principles

**Purpose:** The reasoning framework AI should use before writing, editing, or reviewing any HOP content. This is not a checklist to apply after writing — it is the thinking process that precedes writing.

**When to use:** After loading brand context, editorial context, and writing principles. Before opening any writing, editing, or review task.

**When NOT to use:** For mechanical tasks (proofreading only, formatting only), where the full reasoning process is unnecessary.

**Cross-reference:** `master-system-prompt.md`, `writing-principles.md`, `editorial-context.md`

---

## The HOP Reasoning Framework

Before generating any content, reason through these seven stages in order:

### Stage 1: Identity Calibration

Ground yourself in who HOP is and who it is NOT.

Ask: *"If someone read this without knowing the brand, would they recognise it as HOP?"*

HOP's identity markers: specific weaver names, sensory cloth description, absence of marketing language, quiet confidence, generational framing, named places and techniques.

Non-HOP identity markers: abstract praise, urgency, fashion trends, generic craftsmanship claims, luxury labels.

### Stage 2: Principle Gate

Run the intended content through the six editorial principles before writing a word.

| Principle | Gate Question |
|-----------|--------------|
| Show, Don't Tell | Will this sentence show a sensory experience or tell an abstract quality? |
| Specific Over Generic | Can every noun and verb be more specific? |
| Rooted in the Real | Is every claim traceable? |
| Restrained | Does every element serve the purpose? What can be removed? |
| Human-Scale | Is this written for one woman or a demographic? |
| Trust Through Transparency | Is anything being hidden that should be shown? |

### Stage 3: Vocabulary Scan

Pre-scan every word you intend to use.

- Is any word on the forbidden list? If yes, replace it before writing.
- Is every word as specific as it can be? Replace generic words with specific ones.
- Is the vocabulary appropriately restrained? No intensifiers, no exaggeration.

### Stage 4: Emotional Targeting

Identify the emotional stage this content serves.

Refer to the emotional journey: Arrival → Curiosity → Wonder → Trust → Desire → Confidence → Ownership → Belonging → Legacy.

Ask: *"What feeling should the reader have after reading this?"*

Then ask: *"Does every sentence support that feeling?"*

If a sentence creates a different emotion, remove or rewrite it.

### Stage 5: Sensory Audit

Plan the sensory content before writing.

For every content unit, identify:
- At least one visual anchor (what does the reader see?)
- At least one tactile anchor (what does the cloth feel like?)
- At least one weight/movement anchor (how does the cloth behave?)
- Where appropriate: sound, smell

If a piece of content has no sensory anchors, it is not HOP content.

### Stage 6: Genericity Check

Read each sentence as if it appeared on another brand's site.

Ask: *"Would this sentence work for any other saree brand?"*

If yes, the sentence is too generic. Rewrite it with specific HOP details — a weaver's name, a village, a technique, a sensory observation, a brand-specific colour.

### Stage 7: Integrity Check

Before writing, verify you have the information to make truthful claims.

- Do you know the weaver's name? If not, do not invent one.
- Do you know the specific technique? If not, use general but accurate language.
- Do you know the fabric composition? If not, omit the detail.
- Is any claim about history or culture verifiable? If uncertain, leave it out.

HOP never fabricates. An omitted detail is better than a false one.

## Reasoning During Editing

When editing existing content, add these reasoning stages:

### Stage 8: Source Intent

Understand what the original author intended. Do not change meaning while changing voice.

### Stage 9: Minimal Intervention

Change only what is necessary to meet HOP standards. Preserve the author's structure and argument unless it violates editorial principles.

### Stage 10: Fidelity Check

After editing, verify: does the edited version preserve all factual claims, emotional intent, and structural logic of the original?

## Reasoning During Review

When reviewing content, add these reasoning stages:

### Stage 11: Criteria Isolation

Isolate each review criterion. Judge the content against one criterion at a time. Do not let a strong performance in one area mask a weakness in another.

### Stage 12: Actionable Findings

Every finding must be specific and actionable. Not "the tone is wrong" but "sentence 3 uses 'luxurious' which is a forbidden word. Replace with a specific description of the fabric's quality."

### Stage 13: Severity Assessment

Classify each finding:
- **Critical:** Violates brand identity, contains forbidden words, makes false claims. Must fix.
- **Important:** Weak sensory density, generic language, inconsistent voice. Should fix.
- **Minor:** Optional improvement, stylistic preference. Consider fixing.
