# Writing Principles

**Purpose:** HOP-specific writing rules that guide how AI constructs sentences, paragraphs, and narratives. These operationalise the voice bible and editorial principles into direct writing instructions.

**When to use:** After loading brand and editorial context, before executing any writing task. Also used by editing documents to evaluate existing writing.

**When NOT to use:** For strategic content decisions (what to write), use `writing/` documents. For identity questions, use `brand-context.md`.

## Sentence Construction

### Rhythm Pattern

```
Short sentence. (8-12 words)
Medium sentence that extends the thought. (12-18 words)
Longer sentence that adds a detail or sensory observation. (18-25 words)
Short sentence that lands. (8-12 words)
```

### Rules

- No two consecutive sentences with the same structure
- Vary openings: subject, adverb, prepositional phrase, dependent clause
- End sentences with content words (nouns, verbs), not prepositions or weak adverbs
- Avoid there-is/there-are openings
- Avoid it-is openings without a clear referent

## Paragraph Construction

- 2-4 sentences per paragraph
- One idea per paragraph
- No transition paragraphs — if the idea changes, start a new paragraph
- First line: short and declarative
- Last line: resolves the paragraph's thought

## Sensory Writing

Every content unit must contain at least one sentence per relevant sense:

| Sense | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Sight | "The zari catches the morning light — not the harsh noon light, but the soft gold of early day." |
| Touch | "The fabric settles at the hip with a weight that says 'I am here.'" |
| Sound | "The rhythm of the shuttle — back and forth, back and forth." |
| Weight | "The weight of pure mulberry silk, twelve momme, dense enough to hold its drape." |
| Movement | "The pallu falls over the shoulder like water finding its level." |

## Specificity Rules

### The Provenance Test

Every claim must answer: can this be traced to a specific place, person, technique, or moment?

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "Our sarees are made by skilled artisans." | "Woven by Gangamma in Molakalmuru, on a pit loom her grandmother used." |
| "Premium quality fabric." | "Mulberry silk, 12-momme, hand-dyed in small batches." |
| "A special occasion saree." | "This saree belongs to winter weddings where the light fades by five." |

### Naming Rules

- Name the weaver whenever possible
- Name the village or town, not just the state
- Name the specific weave or technique, not just the category
- Name the time of day, not just "morning"
- Name the exact colour using the brand palette

## Restraint Rules

- One precise adjective instead of two generic ones
- One primary CTA per page
- Show trust signals on demand (accordion), not by default
- No "you might also like" at the end of journal articles
- No transition words between paragraphs (however, moreover, furthermore)
- No TM, (R), or (C) symbols

## Human-Scale Writing

- Address one woman, not a demographic
- Use "you" and "your" when addressing the reader
- Use "she" and "her" when describing the wearer
- Never use "customer," "consumer," "buyer," or "user"
- Include at least one human detail: a name, a relationship, a memory, a preference

## Opening and Closing Rules

### Openings

- Start with a specific moment, object, or observation
- Never open with a question
- Never open with "At House of Padmavati, we..."
- Never open with a general statement about the world

### Closings

- End with a sentence that resolves and resonates
- Never close with a question
- Never close with a CTA in editorial content
- The last sentence should leave the reader in a quiet place
- Prefer a conclusion over a fade

## Formatting

- Sentence case for all headlines
- No exclamation marks
- No all-caps for emphasis
- Em dashes with spaces for parenthetical thoughts
- Curly quotes throughout
- Contractions allowed but used consistently
